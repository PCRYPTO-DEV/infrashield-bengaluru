# First customer: land and project teams in Gurugram

City Atlas tried to serve families, investors, developers, city officials and advertisers at once. This page picks **one** buyer and says how we prove we are worth paying for.

## Who

**The land-acquisition and project teams of real-estate developers working in Gurugram.**

- **Customer zero is ATBOSE itself** (Atbose Projects & Development, Gurugram). We use City Atlas on our own land and project decisions first.
- **Next: 5 developers or channel partners in Gurugram and the Dwarka Expressway / New Gurgaon belt.** These are teams that look at 10–50 sites a year.

Why them, and not the others:

| Option | Why not first |
|---|---|
| Families / consumers | They don't pay for maps; no revenue to learn from |
| PE funds, banks, insurers | They need years of validated, price-linked accuracy we don't have yet |
| Smart cities / police | Long tenders; they need audited data |
| OOH advertisers (AIRAD) | Needs footfall data we don't have |
| **Gurugram developers** | **We are one. Decisions are local and frequent. What they ask (is the area changing? is it connected? how much is coming up nearby? is anyone selling under stress?) matches what Atlas measures with real data.** |

## The job they hire City Atlas for

> "Before we bid for this plot, tell me in 10 minutes whether this locality is on the way up, what's being built around it, and what could go wrong, with sources I can show my boss."

## What they get (Pro)

1. **Gentrification** for the plot: stage, community score, two years of amenity momentum, neighbours, civic advisory.
2. **INVEST score** (UINTEL+), with their own asking price, rent and permitted FAR.
3. **Place card**: connectivity, services, green, air and live traffic.
4. **Track record**: how often Atlas's predictions came true (below).
5. **Client report** to print or forward.

Pro is labelled in the app as **"for land & project teams"**.

## How we prove it: the Track record

Buyers trust a score that has been checked. Every check below runs on the live server against what actually happened, and **misses are shown next to hits**.

| Prediction | How it is checked | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Amenity momentum (gentrification) | For 28 Gurugram localities: hide the last 12 months of OpenStreetMap history, predict them from the 24 months before, compare with what was mapped. Reported next to a "nothing changes" guess. | **Running on Render** (`/api/trackrecord`, *More → track record*) |
| Traffic "usual for this hour" | Each recent reading is compared with the median for that weekday and hour, using only earlier readings | Running; needs a few weeks of memory |
| Prices and rents | Needs a price source | **Not checked**: no source |
| INVEST score | Needs real deal outcomes | **Not checked**: needs ATBOSE's own deals (see below) |

The outcome checked today is **mapped premium places, not price**. That is said everywhere it is shown. Turning this into "our score predicted price growth" needs price data, which is the next step.

## Next 90 days

1. **Weeks 1–2:** ATBOSE runs 10 of its own past and current sites through Pro. We write down what Atlas said, what the team decided, and what happened.
2. **Weeks 2–4:** collect ATBOSE's own transaction and quote history (locality, date, ₹/sq ft). This becomes the first price outcome to check the INVEST score and gentrification against. Their data stays private.
3. **Weeks 4–8:** show the Track record and 3 site reports to 5 Gurugram developers or channel partners. Offer a paid pilot per site report or per month.
4. **Weeks 8–12:** keep what pilots used; cut what they ignored. Only then add a second city or a second buyer.

## What we stop doing for now

- No new consumer features beyond the five stones.
- No new cities beyond Gurugram/NCR for the paid product. The free map stays India-wide.
- No GCCOS, DRISHTI or AIRAD work until real data adapters exist.

## Success looks like

- A Track record page that shows real hits and misses for Gurugram.
- 3 paying pilot customers.
- At least one decision where the team says "Atlas changed what we did, and it was right".
